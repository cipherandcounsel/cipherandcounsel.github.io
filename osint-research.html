Volt Typhoon
OSINT Threat Actor Investigation

Living-off-the-Land Pre-Positioning Against U.S. Critical Infrastructure

Volt Typhoon, also tracked as Vanguard Panda, BRONZE SILHOUETTE, Insidious Taurus, Dev-0391, UNC3236, and Voltzite, is a People's Republic of China (PRC) state-sponsored cyber actor. U.S. agencies assess with high confidence that it is pre-positioning inside U.S. critical infrastructure networks to enable the disruption of operational technology in a major crisis or conflict. The group rarely uses malware for post-compromise execution. It relies on living-off-the-land (LotL) techniques and a botnet of compromised small-office / home-office (SOHO) routers to blend in with normal network activity, and U.S. agencies report access lasting at least five years in some victim IT environments. This investigation consolidates reporting from CISA, the NSA, the FBI, the Department of Justice, and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence into a multi-year timeline, a government-sourced MITRE ATT&CK technique mapping, and behavioral detection guidance for defenders.

What You Need to Know

KVbotnet
Hundreds of end-of-life SOHO routers hijacked as covert proxy infrastructure, disrupted by a court-authorized FBI operation announced Jan 31, 2024

Volt Typhoon is a Chinese state-sponsored actor that U.S. agencies assess is pre-positioning inside the networks that run America's communications, energy, water, and transportation systems. What makes it distinct is intent: the access is meant to be in place if a future crisis demands disruption.

Because the group rarely uses malware and works mostly with legitimate, built-in administrative tools, traditional antivirus and signature-based defenses rarely catch it. The intrusions resemble normal IT activity. Detection requires watching behavior: who is logging in, from where, and doing what, rather than scanning for malicious files.

Campaign Timeline

A Multi-Year Pre-Positioning Campaign

The public record, drawn from U.S. government sources, runs from long-term quiet access, to a joint advisory, to a law enforcement disruption of the group's proxy infrastructure, to continued government warnings through 2026.

01
Years before 2024
Long-Term Quiet Access
U.S. agencies report Volt Typhoon actors maintaining access and footholds within some victim IT environments for at least five years. Actors gain entry by exploiting vulnerabilities in networking appliances from Fortinet, Ivanti, NETGEAR, Citrix, and Cisco, then rely mainly on valid credentials to persist.
02
May 2023
Joint Government Advisory AA23-144A
CISA, NSA, FBI, and Five Eyes partners publish a joint advisory on PRC state-sponsored activity that uses living-off-the-land techniques to evade detection. The February 2024 advisory describes May 2023 as the first disclosure of related activity.
03
January 2024
KV Botnet Disruption
The U.S. Department of Justice announces on January 31, 2024 a court-authorized operation in which the FBI deleted KV Botnet malware from hundreds of U.S.-based end-of-life SOHO routers, mostly Cisco and NETGEAR models, and took steps to block their connection to the botnet. DOJ states that Volt Typhoon used the botnet to conceal its hacking of critical infrastructure.
04
February 2024
CISA Advisory AA24-038A
CISA, NSA, and the FBI, with DOE, EPA, TSA, and international partners, publish a joint advisory dated February 7, 2024. It reports access in some victim IT environments for at least five years and assesses with high confidence that the actors are pre-positioning to enable the disruption of operational technology functions in a major crisis or conflict.
05
2026
Continued Government Warnings
The March 2026 ODNI Annual Threat Assessment states that China is the most active and persistent cyber threat to U.S. Government, private-sector, and critical infrastructure networks. A joint advisory published April 23, 2026 by the NSA, partner agencies in the United Kingdom, Australia, and Germany, and others (CISA AA26-113A) describes China-nexus actors, including Volt Typhoon, using large covert networks of compromised SOHO and IoT devices to hide their activity.

Executive Recommendations

What to Do Now

Priority 01
Assume Compromise and Hunt for Behavior, Not Malware
Because Volt Typhoon uses built-in tools, not malware, signature-based defenses are insufficient. Prioritize threat hunting against authentication logs, command-line activity, and administrative tool usage to find access that antivirus will never flag.
Priority 02
Patch and Harden Internet-Facing Appliances
Initial access depends on vulnerable edge devices: VPNs, firewalls, and routers. Apply vendor patches promptly, remove end-of-life hardware from service, and disable unnecessary management interfaces exposed to the internet.
Priority 03
Enforce Phishing-Resistant MFA and Credential Hygiene
The group escalates by stealing valid administrator credentials, including domain controller databases. Enforce phishing-resistant MFA, rotate exposed credentials, and tightly restrict and monitor privileged accounts.
Priority 04
Secure and Monitor the IT / OT Boundary
Pre-positioning aims at operational technology that runs physical infrastructure. Segment IT from OT networks, monitor traffic crossing that boundary, and assume lateral movement is the adversary's goal.
Strategic Context

The February 2024 CISA / NSA / FBI advisory assessed with high confidence that Volt Typhoon is pre-positioning on IT networks to enable the disruption of operational technology functions in the event of a major crisis or conflict. The threat is strategic and persistent: the objective is access that can be used later. A quiet network is not evidence of safety.

Actor Overview

Volt Typhoon: Group Profile

Volt Typhoon is a People's Republic of China state-sponsored cyber group that CISA, NSA, and the FBI assess with high confidence is conducting pre-positioning operations against U.S. critical infrastructure. Also tracked as Vanguard Panda, BRONZE SILHOUETTE, Insidious Taurus, Dev-0391, UNC3236, and Voltzite, the group has compromised organizations primarily in the communications, energy, transportation, and water and wastewater sectors, including in U.S. territories such as Guam. Agencies report access lasting at least five years in some victim IT environments.

Volt Typhoon rarely uses malware for post-compromise execution. Operators rely on built-in administrative utilities, including wmic, ntdsutil, netsh, PowerShell, and certutil, for discovery, credential access, and lateral movement, with limited use of open-source tools such as Fast Reverse Proxy (FRP). Command and control has been routed through multi-hop proxies, including a botnet of compromised SOHO network devices, which obscures the origin of the activity.

Infrastructure Analysis

The KV Botnet & Edge-Device Proxying

Covert SOHO Router Proxy Network

Volt Typhoon has used multi-hop proxies for command and control. These are typically built from virtual private servers, and in some cases from compromised SOHO routers: the KV Botnet, made up of hundreds of U.S.-based routers, the vast majority of them end-of-life Cisco and NETGEAR models that no longer received security updates. Proxying through compromised routers obscures where the malicious activity originates.

Law-Enforcement Disruption, January 2024

On January 31, 2024 the U.S. Department of Justice announced a court-authorized operation in which the FBI deleted the KV Botnet malware from the compromised routers and took steps to sever their connection to the botnet. DOJ states that the operation did not affect the routers' legitimate functions or collect their content. The operation targeted the routers. Access already held inside victim enterprise networks is a separate remediation problem.

Detection Guidance

Behavioral Detection & Mitigation

Identity & Credential Controls

Detection
  • Alert on anomalous use of valid administrator accounts: off-hours logins, new source ASNs, or impossible-travel patterns that do not match the account's history
  • Monitor for ntdsutil execution and Volume Shadow Copy creation on domain controllers, a high-fidelity signal of NTDS.dit theft
  • Enforce phishing-resistant MFA on all privileged and remote-access accounts; rotate credentials known to have been exposed on compromised appliances

Endpoint & Command-Line Telemetry

Detection
  • Enable and centrally collect command-line and PowerShell script-block logging. LotL activity is invisible without process-level telemetry
  • Baseline normal administrative tool usage and alert on wmic, netsh portproxy, and certutil invocations outside known workflows
  • Hunt for fileless execution chains spawning from unexpected parent processes on servers and edge hosts

Network & Edge Controls

Detection
  • Patch and replace internet-facing appliances promptly; retire end-of-life routers and firewalls that can be conscripted as proxy infrastructure
  • Monitor for unexpected port-proxy and tunneling configurations and outbound connections from servers that should not initiate them
  • Segment IT from OT and inspect traffic crossing that boundary, because pre-positioning aims at the operational systems behind it
